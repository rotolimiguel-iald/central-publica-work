ABSTRACT 
The type la supernova SN 1994D in NGC 4526 was extensively observed as a target 
of opportunity at ESO. In this paper, we present and discuss the UBVR1 photometry 
and the spectra collected during this campaign from 11 days before maximum to 
about two months afterwards. 
Several absorption components of the Na i D lines arising in our Galaxy, in NGC 
4526 and in high-velocity clouds located in between have been identified in a high- 
resolution spectrum. The total amount of reddening related to these features has been 
estimated as E{B- V ) = 0.06. 
The light curve shapes are similar, but not identical, to those of SN 1992A. 
The onset of the secondary maximum in the I band occurs around day + 20. 
The SN reached at maximum 5 = 11.84 and showed a distinctly blue colour 
[{B-V) = -0.08]. SN 1994D is therefore the bluest SN la observed to date. SN 
1994D appears to depart from the previously reported correlation between 
maximum brightness and rate of decay. The implications of this for the absolute 
magnitudes and colour dispersion of type la SNe are discussed. 
The spectroscopic evolution of SN 1994D followed that of standard objects like 
SN 1992A. The excellent time coverage allowed us to show a sudden break in the 
decline rate of the expansion velocity deduced from the Si n 26355 line at around day 
- 6, probably indicating that after this phase this strong feature originates well above 
the photosphere. Finally, physical parameters and line identifications have been 
obtained modelling the day - 4 spectrum by means of a spectrum synthesis code. 
Overall, the spectrum is successfully fitted as a normal SN la, although the very blue 
colour cannot be reproduced. The observed weakness of the Co n absorption around 
3350 Â and its possible bearing on the amount of radioactive Ni produced during the 
explosion are discussed.

1 INTRODUCTION 
SN 1994D (a = 12h 34m 02^37, 0= +7° 42' 04'.'7, 2000.0) 
was discovered on 1994 March 7 in NGC 4526 9 arcsec 
west and 7 arcsec north of the galaxy nucleus, during the 
Leuschner Observatory Supernova Search (Treffers, 
Filippenko & Van Dyk 1994). On the basis of a spectrum 
taken two days later, and showing a very blue continuum and 
©1996 RAS 
the characteristic Si h A6355 feature, this supernova (SN) 
was classified as type la well in advance of maximum bright- 
ness (Martel & Goodrich 1994). 
Three reasons provided the motivation for a special effort 
in the observational coverage of this SN: (i) the parent galaxy 
belongs to the Virgo cluster, which represents a crucial step 
in the distance ladder; (ii) the absence of spectral peculiari- 
ties of the kind shown by two recent Virgo SNe la, 199IT

The goal of obtaining a daily coverage of the spectro-

In Fig. 1 the positions of SN 1994D and of the two refer-

Colour terms for each instrument have been computed 
using standard field observations (Landolt 1992). The SN 
magnitudes have been calibrated by means of relative photo- 
metry with respect to star A (see Fig. 1), whose UBVRI 
magnitudes were given by Kilkenny & Malcom (1984). A 
calibration of this star has been checked on two photometric 
nights (1994 March 10 and 12) giving values (£7=14.58, 
B= 13.59, V= 12.54, Æ = 12.01 and 7=11.50) in good 
agreement with those reported by Kilkenny & Malcom 
(1984). To estimate the internal errors we used star B. The 
magnitudes of this star, averaged over 18 nights, are 
£7=16.74 (cr= 0.06), £ = 16.78 (a = 0.04), F= 16.19

3 INTERSTELLAR ABSORPTION TOWARDS 
SN 1994D 
Since SN 1994D is seen projected on to dust lane of the 
parent galaxy NGC 4526 (see Fig. 1), the presence of inter- 
stellar extinction is not unexpected. Indeed, as shown in Fig. 
2, in the high-resolution spectrum of SN 1994D we detected 
a number of unresolved components of the Na i D doublet at 
velocities ^LSr= “9.9, 212.3, 227.7, 249.5 and 708.9 km 
s" ^ in good agreement with the similar report by King et al. 
(1995). Another component, barely resolved from that at 
212.3 km s_1, is visible at ^^ = 203.5 km s_1, at a velocity 
coincident with that of one component of the Ca n H and K 
doublet observed by King et al. (1995). While the lower 
velocity component is associated with the Galaxy and the 
higher velocity component with NGC 4526, the intermediate 
components have been attributed to high-velocity clouds 
(HVC) located at distances between 1 and 10 kpc (King et al. 
1995). 
The total equivalent widths (EW) of the Na i D lines are 
39 mÄ for the galactic component, 89 mÀ for the HVCs, 
and 76 mÀ for the interstellar gas in NGC 4526. The total 
EW (Na i D) is 0.20 Â. The empirical relation between EW 
(Na i D) and reddening given by Richmond et al. (1994) gives 
E{B- V ) - 0.08, while the relation given by Barbon et al. 
(1990) gives E(B- F)~0.05, in excellent agreement with 
the estimates of King et al. (1995), E{B - F ) ~ 0.05, obtained 
from the neutral hydrogen column density. Therefore we 
adopt as a best estimate of the reddening towards SN 1994D 
a value of E{B - F ) ~ 0.06 ± 0.02.

tion of a very faint feature at about 6446 Á, which can be 
identified with the diffuse interstellar band (DIB) 6445 À

7 A SYNTHETIC SPECTRUM 
We have modelled the March 17 spectrum of SN 1994D 
using the Monte Carlo code described by Mazzali & Lucy 
(1993), which was already successfully used to model the 
SNe la 1990N (Mazzali et al. 1993) and 1991T (Mazzali, 
Danziger & Turatto 1995). 
The spectrum corresponds to an epoch 4 days before B 
maximum, which can be considered as a typical representa- 
tion of an SN la photospheric epoch spectrum. At this epoch 
SN 1994D had magnitudes t/= 11.41, 5 = 11.99, F= 12.08, 
i.e. {B-V)=- 0.09. For our models, we assumed a rise time 
to B maximum of 20 days, so that on March 17 the epoch 
from the explosion is 16 d. We used the reddening esti- 
mated in Section ?>[E(B — V) = 0.06] and we have adopted as 
distance modulus the value ^ = 30.68 mag (i.e. 13.7 Mpc), 
measured with the SBF method (Tonry 1995). 
The similarity between SNe 1994D and 1990N leads us 
rather straightforwardly to the determination of the other 
input parameters for the synthetic spectrum. These are: 
L= 1.10 x 1043 erg s_1 (i.e. log L/L0 = 9.46, MBoi= -18.9) 
and ^ph= 10 500 km s"1, i.e. Æph= 1.45 x 1015 cm (or

composition was that of W7 with full mixing for 
v > 6000 km s~\ The relative abundances of Fe, Co and Ni

the equivalent blackbody temperature at the photosphere is

beyond at phase - 4 days, so that Co is more concentrated in 
the innermost layers that are not yet visible at this epoch. 
Since the iron produced in the explosion should be closely 
coupled to the cobalt, one should try to answer the question 
of whether or not the amount of iron in the envelope 
modelled through the various Fe ii and Fe m transitions can 
be made compatible with the (small) amount of cobalt 
required to fit the Co n feature. Since this is not the case, the 
fines of Fe can only be well reproduced if it is assumed that 
the fraction of 54Fe is enhanced with respect to the W7 
model. Then one is still left with the question of why there is 
mixing in some supernovae and not in others, a matter to be 
addressed with better understood models of the explosion 
and its immediate aftermath. Another possibility is that at the 
moment we have not adequately accounted for all the fine 
opacity in these objects particularly at UV wavelengths. The 
fact that the UV spectra of SN 1990N at early phases differ 
quite strongly from those of SN 1992A (Kirshner et al. 1993) 
suggests there is still much that is unaccounted for in the 
models with respect to the sources of fine opacity. Most of 
the problem may lie with the necessity for a correct treat- 
ment of ionization and excitation in the part of the UV which 
is optically thin in the continuum, but thick in the fines 
due to line blocking.

somewhat different input parameters for the adopted

and none had colours bluer than (Æ - F ) = 0.0. In practice, at 
higher temperatures (i.e. higher L’s or smaller f ph’s), the con-

happens in the hot SN 199IT (Mazzali et al. 1995). Perhaps 
it is the somewhat high flux in the red part of the model 
which keeps the colours too red. Since this flux is the result 
of photon blocking in the UV, this may signal that we are 
overestimating that effect, although it is not easy to see how 
less blocking could result in a smaller V flux but leave U and

8 DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS 
In this paper we presented the optical observations of SN 
1994D collected at ESO and Asiago, which provide an 
almost daily coverage and constitute one of the largest set of 
observations of a SN la near maximum light. 
High-resolution (0.2 À FWHM at 5900 Â) spectroscopy 
allowed the detection of three components in the Na i D 
doublet owing to clouds in the Galaxy, to HVCs and to inter- 
stellar matter within the parent galaxy (Section 3). Although 
the parent galaxy, NGC 4526, is of early type (SO), this is not 
surprising, since the SN exploded on the edge of a dust lane. 
Empirical relations EW (Nai D) versus E{B- V) give a 
reddening £(Æ-F) = 0.06 in good agreement with an 
analogous finding by King et al. (1995) based on the Hi 
column density. 
The frequent and accurate UBVRI photometric coverage 
allows a detailed study of the photometric evolution. In the U 
band the peak of the light curve was narrow. B maximum, 
which has been assumed as reference for the computation of 
the epochs, was reached 2 days later. In these bands maxi- 
mum light was followed by a normal luminosity decline. V 
maximum, which was reached 2 days after B maximum, as is 
normal for SNe la, is less well defined, and the luminosity 
declines more slowly. This effect is even more pronounced at 
redder wavelengths {R and /), where the peaks are flat 
topped and secondary maxima (perhaps hinted also in V) 
are visible about 20 days past the principal maxima. The 
photometric data of SN 1994D are summarized in Table 3. 
The presence of secondary maxima in the JHK light 
curves of normal SNe la had already been noticed earlier by 
Elias et al. ( 1981 ) who also noted a similar behaviour in the / 
(and possibly R) band of SN 1972E. Our new data on SN 
1994D, along with other recently published observations of 
SN 1989B (Wells et al. 1994) and SN 1992A (Suntzeff 1995) 
confirm previous findings based on poorer quality material. 
It seems clear from the evidence adduced by Suntzeff 
(1995), and from our VRI photometry for SN 1994D and 
associated spectrophotometry, that the double-peaked light 
curves have little to do with the presence of absorption 
features, or the lack of emission features. Rather, they appear 
to result from a redistribution of energy over a considerable 
wavelength range as a result of which longer wavelength 
radiation is enhanced at the expense of shorter wavelength 
radiation. The physical reason for this at a phase 20 days 
after maximum light remains to be elucidated. Höflich (1995) 
has been able, with some classes of models, to reproduce this 
secondary maximum as a result of significant expansion of 
the photospheric radius after primary maximum. Clearly, 
closely spaced observations of SN la light curves over the 
complete accessible wavelength range are desirable. 
With the available photometry, the colour evolution can 
also be followed in great detail. The comparison with SN 
1992A, an object in many respects very similar to SN 
1994D, shows real differences at maximum light, which 
cannot be attributed to uncertainties in the measurements. 
We stress also that the intrinsic colour of the SN is consider- 
ably blue, {B - F)giax= -0.14. This value, compared with 
those of other well-studied SNe la, confirms the colour dis- 
persion within this class of objects. 
SN 1994D is among the brightest SNe observed in the 
Virgo cluster (cf. Table 4). Whether this is a result of the

The spectral evolution in the photospheric epochs 
described here follows the path of normal SNe la, such as SN 
1992A (cf. Fig. 8). The coverage starts very early, spanning 
from day - 11 to day +55; this allowed a detailed study of 
the line position and profile variations. In particular, we 
analysed the behaviour of the Si n A6355 line. The evolution 
of the expansion velocity deduced from the absorption mini- 
mum of this feature is similar to those shown by SNe 1990N 
and 1989B, while it is slightly different from SNe 198 IB and 
1992A, SN 1994D being ~ 1000 km s-1 slower. It shows a 
sudden break in the decline slope at around day - 6, which is 
observed also in the Ca n H and K lines. This is probably a 
common feature for all the strong lines, which are formed 
with the contribution of envelope layers even well above the 
photosphere, spanning a large range of velocities. This is not 
the case for weaker lines, which form close to the photo- 
sphere. For this reason such weak lines should be used to 
estimate the photospheric velocity. This is an important 
point concerning the use of the expanding photosphere 
method in estimating SN distances. The comparison between 
the observed values of the velocity and those derived from 
spectral modelling (cf. Fig. 11 ) confirms this behaviour. 
We modelled the day - 4 spectrum of SN 1994D using a 
Monte Carlo spectral synthesis code. The adopted density 
structure and abundances are drawn from a classical mixed 
W7 model. Most features are well reproduced assuming that 
the burst occurred 20 days before B maximum, and had a 
photospheric velocity of 10 500 km s"1 and an effective tem- 
perature of 9300 K, in fair agreement with the analogous 
values found for SN 1990N at a similar epoch. In particular, 
the positions and intensities of the Si n, S n, Fe n and Fe m 
lines are satisfactorily matched. The model does not succeed 
in reproducing the observed colours and the spectral feature 
at 3300 Â, which is a good diagnostic for the Co abundance. 
We note that other objects which are otherwise similar to SN 
1994D (SNe 1990N and 1992A) show at this epoch differ- 
ent profiles for these lines. Further spectral modelling of the 
whole data set is mandatory in order to assess if this is caused 
by an overall underabundance of Ni in SN 1994D (which is 
in conflict with the apparently normal luminosity and the 
blue colour) or by a deviation from the standard W7 abun-
