ABSTRACT 
We present a family of six BVI template light curves for SNe la for days -5 and +80, based on high-quality 
data gathered at CTIO. These templates display a wide range of light-curve morphologies, with initial 
decline rates of their B light curves between Arn15(B) = 0.87m and 1.93w. We use these templates to study 
the general morphology of SNe la light curves. We find that several of the main features of the BVI 
templates correlate tightly with Am15(Z?). In particular, the V light curves, which are probably a reasonably 
good approximation of the bolometric fight curves, display an orderly progression in shapes between the 
most-luminous, slowest-declining events and the least-luminous, fastest-declining SNe. This supports the 
idea that the observed spectroscopic and photometric sequences of SNe la are due primarily to one 
parameter. Nevertheless, SNe with very similar initial decline rates do show significant differences in their 
fight curve properties when examined in detail, suggesting the influence of one or more secondary 
parameters. © 1996 American Astronomical Society.

Bergh & Pazder 1992; Sandage & Tammann 1993). A major

city of photometric data. An inspection of the atlas of SNe la 
fight curves (Leibundgut et al 1991) reveals that most of the

The B template light curves are characterized by a rapid

is located between 7 and 21 days after maximum. We call

derivative of the B light curve reaches a maximum. Another 
useful time parameter is given by the intersection of the 
straight fine that fits the exponential tail and the straight line 
that fits the initial decline phase of the B light curve. These 
two fits are shown as dotted lines in Fig. 8 and we call this

can be considered as the onset of the exponential tail. An-

the linear tail (relative to the peak) which we measure here

5. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS 
The set of templates presented in this paper were selected 
to be representative of the range of observed SNe la light 
curve morphologies. During the first —100 days following 
maximum light, the bolometric light curves of SNe la are 
fairly well approximated by the V light curve (e.g., see 
Suntzeff 1996). Focusing on the V light curves displayed in 
Fig. 9, there appears to be a orderly progression from the 
most-luminous, slowly-declining events like SNe 1991 T and 
1992bc, to the least-luminous, fastest-declining SNe such as 
92bo/93H and 1991bg. The strong correlations between 
and the two V light curve decline rate parameters 
Am2o(V) and Am60(V) (see Figs. 11 and 12) strengthens the 
impression that the light curve shapes are governed primarily 
by a single parameter, consistent with the suggestion by Nu- 
gent et al (1995) that the observed spectroscopic and photo- 
metric sequences of SNe la are due primarily to one param- 
eter. These authors concluded that the parameter was most 
likely the mass of radioactive 56Ni produced in the explosion. 
Assuming that the physical conditions in the ejecta of the 
different SNe during the final exponential decline phase are 
similar, the different luminosities during this phase could be 
interpreted as reflecting differing 56Ni masses. Pinto & East- 
man (1996) have argued that the relative factor is actually the 
mass of the progenitor white dwarf star, with masses in the 
range 0.5-1.4 required to explain the range of observed 
luminosities and decline rates. 
Nevertheless, a detailed comparison of the templates of 
the two slowest-declining, most-luminous SNe la, 1992bc 
and 199IT, shows that one or more additional parameters 
probably influence the light curve shapes. This is seen most 
clearly by comparing the B and I light curves of these two 
events (see Fig. 7). Although the initial decline rates of 
1992bc and 199IT as measured by both Aml5(B) and 
Am2o(V) are very similar, this phase lasts significantly 
longer in 1992bc (t2 occurred 6 days later for 1992bc than it 
did for 199IT). This, in turn leads to the quite different val- 
ues of Am60(B) observed for these two SNe. We note thaï 
while the premaximum spectrum of SN 1992bc was very 
similar to other “typical” SNe la (Hamuy et al, in prepara- 
tion), SN 199IT was an abnormal object with a variety ol 
premaximum spectroscopic pecuharities (Filippenko et al 
1992a; Phillips et al 1992). Another case in point is SN 
1990N, which displayed decline rates in B and V that were 
essentially identical to those of SN 1992al, but whose values 
of Ara60(5), Am60(U), and Am60(/) were more similar tc 
those of the slower-declining SN 199IT. These small differ- 
ences could be due to variations in parameters such as the 
explosion energy/mechanism and/or the distribution of 56Ni 
in the ejecta. 
Finally, we consider the fastest-declining event in oui 
sample, SN 1991bg. From Fig. 7, one might conclude thaï 
this SN does not “fit in” well with the other SNe la since the 
final exponential tail of the B light curve sets in very early, 
causing the template to cross the other template curves. This 
same property is responsible for the discrepant position oi 
1991bg in the top panels of Figs. 12 and 13. However, in 
Fig. 9 which is a more physically-meaningful plot of the 
templates, 199 Ibg does not appear to be nearly so discor- 
dant. Note in particular that the exponential decline phase ol 
the B light curve does not cross the other template curves. 
The low luminosity of this SN in the B band and its unusu- 
ally red B — V color at maximum (Filippenko et al 1992b; 
Leibundgut et al 1993) were almost certainly due to a lower 
than “normal” (Nugent et al 1995) effective temperature. 
As mentioned earlier, the bolometric light curves of all ol 
these SNe are probably closest to the V light curves, and the 
V light curve of 199Ibg appears to be a fairly smooth ex- 
trapolation of the other template curves (see Figs. 7 and 9). 
Hence, we conclude that the VRI light curve shapes of SN 
199Ibg are consistent with the idea that a single major pa- 
rameter can explain the major characteristics of the observed 
sequence of light curve morphologies for SNe la.
